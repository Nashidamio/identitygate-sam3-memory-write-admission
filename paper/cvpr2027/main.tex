\title{When Should SAM 3 Write to Memory?\\
A Controlled Study of Signal-Informed Memory Admission for
Video Object Segmentation}

\author{Anonymous CVPR 2027 Submission}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Video object segmentation systems rely on memory to preserve object state
over time, yet not every prediction is equally suitable for future memory.
We study a controlled question: what information should a memory-write gate
use---quality signals, temporal signals, or identity signals?
We introduce a lightweight memory-admission mechanism for a frozen SAM 3
VOS/PVS tracker that physically accepts or blocks candidate memory writes
without fine-tuning the segmentation model.
We evaluate a nested family of manual, learned quality-temporal,
self-identity, and competitor-relative identity policies using
leakage-controlled cohorts, frozen write-rate matching rules, exact-budget
neutral controls, and video-clustered inference.
On the frozen Hard TEST cohort, the preregistered self-identity versus
quality-temporal primary contrast misses the frozen write-rate tolerance
and therefore does not admit a matched-rate primary interpretation.
Among the pre-specified matched secondary comparisons, the learned
quality-temporal gate improves POR@30 over the manual policy by
2.96 percentage points, with a video-clustered BCa 95\% confidence interval
of [0.61, 6.43] percentage points.
The matched competitor-relative versus self-identity comparison yields no
POR@30 difference, providing no established incremental recovery benefit
from the tested relational native-pointer signal.
At the same exact per-video write budgets, the signal-informed policies also
do not establish clear POR@30 superiority over outcome-independent neutral
timing schedules.
These results support learned quality-temporal evidence over the matched
manual policy in the tested setting, while showing that write-rate and
write-budget controls are essential for evaluating memory admission.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Video object segmentation trackers increasingly rely on internal memory to
propagate object state across time.
A memory write is therefore not merely a bookkeeping operation: it changes
the information available to future predictions.
This creates a distinct control problem from frame-wise segmentation itself:
\emph{when should the tracker commit its current state to memory?}

Our study isolates that question in a frozen SAM 3 VOS/PVS substrate.
Rather than modifying the segmentation architecture or fine-tuning the
foundation model, we intervene only on candidate memory admission.
This design permits a controlled comparison of signal families while
preserving the underlying tracker.

The central research question is:
\emph{What information should a memory-write gate use---quality signals,
temporal signals, or identity signals?}

A key methodological difficulty is that different gates can write at
different rates.
A policy that writes less often can appear better or worse because of its
memory budget rather than because it selected more informative states.
We therefore treat realized write rate as an explicit comparison constraint
and complement matched-rate comparisons with exact-budget neutral controls.

The preregistered primary contrast compares the self-identity policy B3-S
against the learned quality-temporal policy B2.  On HARD\_TEST80, their
realized write rates differ by more than the frozen matching tolerance, so
we do not claim a matched-rate primary effect.  Among the pre-specified
matched secondary comparisons, B2 improves post-occlusion recovery over the
manual quality-temporal policy B1.  The clean matched B3-R-versus-B3-S
comparison, however, establishes no incremental recovery benefit from the
tested competitor-relative native-pointer signal.  These findings are
deliberately narrower than claiming that gating universally improves SAM 3
or that identity information is generally unhelpful.

Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item a physically verified closed-loop memory-write intervention for
    frozen SAM 3 VOS/PVS;
    \item a nested evaluation of quality-temporal, self-identity, and
    competitor-relative identity signal families;
    \item a leakage-controlled evaluation protocol with frozen write-rate
    matching requirements and video-clustered bootstrap inference;
    \item exact-budget neutral-selector controls that hold physical write
    counts equal per video when diagnosing signal-informed timing; and
    \item a bounded empirical result: learned quality-temporal gating
    improves recovery over the matched manual policy, while the tested
    native-pointer identity additions establish no incremental recovery
    benefit and the exact-budget neutral diagnostics establish no clear
    superiority of signal-informed timing at the frozen operating points.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Selective memory and reliability.}
Prior VOS work already treats memory management as an explicit design
problem rather than an automatic consequence of propagation.
QDMN learns quality-aware dynamic memory admission in pre-foundation-model
VOS~\cite{QDMN}, while SAMURAI uses motion-aware memory selection in
SAM-family tracking~\cite{SAMURAI}.
DAM4SAM makes distractor-aware memory structure explicit~\cite{DAM4SAM},
and SENTRY validates candidate memory using training-free temporal and
trajectory reasoning~\cite{SENTRY}.
More recent SAM-generation studies further emphasize memory policy:
SAM3-DMS performs per-object reliability-based memory selection in a SAM 3
setting~\cite{SAM3-DMS}, and Rethinking Memory Design studies memory-policy
and memory-architecture choices across SAM-based trackers~\cite{RethinkingMemory}.
OAMVOS provides additional reliability- and occlusion-aware context for
selective memory updates~\cite{OAMVOS}.
These works make a broad \emph{memory filtering is new} claim untenable.
Our question is narrower: with the underlying SAM 3 tracker fixed, which
information is supported for the write-admission decision?

\paragraph{Identity-aware propagation and competitor reasoning.}
Identity information is well established as useful for associating objects
through time.
AOT, DeAOT, and AOST develop object-specific identity representations and
identity-aware propagation mechanisms for VOS~\cite{AOT,DeAOT,AOST}.
Recent systems also combine long-term memory, semantic identity, or
competitor-aware reasoning with modern segmentation models, including
ReMeDI-SAM3, SurgSLOT, CMR, and VOS-Agent
~\cite{ReMeDI-SAM3,SurgSLOT,CMR,VOS-Agent}.
CMR is especially relevant as a competitor-relative memory-readout neighbor,
but it does not implement the same write-admission intervention studied here.
Thus, prior identity-aware tracking motivates the scientific plausibility of
identity signals without establishing that native pointer identity adds
incremental value at write time once quality and temporal evidence are
already available.

\paragraph{Positioning of this study.}
The literature therefore supports all three candidate signal families:
quality, temporal consistency, and identity.
We do not compare unrelated end-to-end systems.
Instead, we hold the SAM 3 VOS/PVS substrate fixed and evaluate a nested
write-side ladder under a common leakage-controlled protocol, explicit
write-rate matching, exact-budget neutral controls, and video-clustered
inference.
This isolates the information available to memory admission rather than
claiming novelty for memory filtering itself.

\section{Controlled Memory-Write Admission}
\label{sec:method}

\begin{figure*}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/method_overview.pdf}
\caption{Overview of the controlled memory-write intervention and

already-produced prediction is preserved while future memory changes.
B0 is the native ungated reference; B1--B3-R vary the information available
to admission; and B5 is the DMS-lite comparator.  Missing identity follows
the frozen B3-R\(\rightarrow\)B3-S\(\rightarrow\)B2 fallback, with
\texttt{pointer\_valid} used only as an availability mask.}
\label{fig:method-overview}
\end{figure*}

Figure~\ref{fig:method-overview} summarizes the intervention and the
signal-family ladder used throughout the experiments.

\subsection{Frozen SAM 3 substrate}

All experiments use the frozen SAM 3 VOS/PVS substrate~\cite{SAM3},
instantiated with \texttt{build\_sam3\_video\_model()} and the upstream
\texttt{facebook/sam3/sam3.pt} checkpoint.  The SAM 3 source is pinned to
commit
\texttt{8f0b7f4d4e7eda2ed606ebde6702c93359ad01da}.
SAM 3 weights and architecture remain fixed: we perform no SAM fine-tuning,
LoRA adaptation, detector modification, or replacement of the native VOS
memory machinery.  The experimental intervention is restricted to whether a
newly produced non-conditioning memory entry is retained for subsequent
propagation.  This keeps the visual predictor fixed while changing only the
information admitted to future memory.

\subsection{Closed-loop admission intervention}

For gated variant \(v\), each tracked object \(o\) at non-conditioning frame
\(t\) produces a deterministic admission score \(s_v(o,t)\) from that
variant's frozen signal set.  We aggregate object-level evidence with the
pre-specified ALL-SAFE rule
\[
    s^{\mathrm{frame}}_v(t)=\min_o s_v(o,t),
\]
and make one frame-level physical decision,
\[
    \mathrm{ADMIT}
    \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
    s^{\mathrm{frame}}_v(t)\geq\tau_v.
\]
Conditioning and prompt frames are always retained.

The physical intervention occurs only after SAM 3 has completed and yielded
the current-frame prediction.  On \textsc{Admit}, the native tracker state is
left unchanged.  On \textsc{Block}, the just-produced frame is removed from
the global \texttt{non\_cond\_frame\_outputs} dictionary and from the
corresponding per-object non-conditioning output dictionaries before the
next propagation step, while normal tracker bookkeeping is retained.
Operationally, blocking is therefore post-yield whole-frame memory eviction:
the decision-frame prediction is preserved, but subsequent propagation uses
a memory state from which that frame is absent.

We verified this intervention separately before evaluating learned gates.
In the mechanism sanity check, every intended blocked frame was present in
both global and per-object memory immediately before eviction and absent
immediately afterward.  The first intervention-frame prediction was
identical to the ungated run, while later masks diverged, demonstrating that
the intervention changes subsequent closed-loop tracking without modifying
the already-produced prediction.  The physical intervention is therefore
frame-level, although its admission score is derived from object-level
signals.

\subsection{Signal-family ladder}

We evaluate a nested ladder designed to vary the information available to
memory admission while keeping the SAM 3 substrate and physical intervention
fixed.

\paragraph{B0: native write behavior.}
B0 is the ungated SAM 3 reference.  It retains every eligible
non-conditioning memory write under the native tracker behavior and therefore
operates at its native physical write rate.

\paragraph{B1: manual quality--temporal rule.}
B1 is a non-learned reliability baseline.  For each object it uses
\texttt{mask\_conf\_iou\_head}, \texttt{occ\_score\_logit},
\texttt{area\_ratio\_anchor}, and \texttt{temporal\_iou\_prev}.  We define
\[
q_{\mathrm{conf}}
  = \operatorname{clip}(\texttt{mask\_conf\_iou\_head},0,1)
    \,\sigma(\texttt{occ\_score\_logit}),
\]
\[
q_{\mathrm{area}} =
\begin{cases}
0, & r=0,\\
\min(r,1/r), & r>0,
\end{cases}
\qquad
q_{\mathrm{temp}}
  = \operatorname{clip}(\texttt{temporal\_iou\_prev},0,1),
\]
where \(r=\texttt{area\_ratio\_anchor}\).
The object score is
\[
s_{\mathrm{B1}}
  = \min(q_{\mathrm{conf}},q_{\mathrm{area}},q_{\mathrm{temp}}).
\]
A non-finite required B1 input fails closed.  B1 has no learned parameters.

\paragraph{B2: learned quality--temporal gate.}
B2 uses five causal quality/temporal inputs:
\texttt{mask\_conf\_iou\_head}, \texttt{occ\_score\_logit},
\texttt{area\_norm}, \texttt{area\_ratio\_anchor}, and
\texttt{temporal\_iou\_prev}.  The gate was trained on TRAIN-only data using
two failure-typed MLP heads with shared TRAIN-only feature normalization.
Each head has hidden dimensions 64 and 32, and the two heads together contain
4,994 learned parameters.  The SAM 3 parameters remain frozen.

\paragraph{B3-S: self-identity augmentation.}
B3-S augments the B2 feature set with
\texttt{ptr\_sim\_anchor\_fp32}, the FP32 cosine similarity between the
current valid object pointer and that object's permanent frame-0 pointer
anchor.  Identity cosine computation disables autocast and TF32 and uses the
highest float32 matrix-multiplication precision.

\paragraph{B3-R: competitor-relative identity augmentation.}
B3-R further adds \texttt{max\_comp\_anchor\_cos\_fp32}, the maximum FP32
cosine similarity between the current valid pointer and the frame-0 anchors
of other tracked objects.  Identity availability is handled by a frozen
nested route: B3-R falls back to B3-S when competitor-relative identity is
unavailable, and B3-S falls back to B2 when self identity is unavailable.
The SAM-derived \texttt{pointer\_valid} flag is used only as an availability
mask and is never supplied as a predictive numeric feature.  Objects are
never removed from the ALL-SAFE frame aggregation because identity is
missing.

\paragraph{B5: DMS-lite write-side comparator.}
B5 is a non-learned comparator inspired by the reliability signal used by
SAM3-DMS~\cite{SAM3-DMS}; it is not an exact reproduction of that system.
For object \(i\), let
\(u_i=\texttt{occ\_score\_logit}_i\) and
\(m_i=\texttt{mask\_conf\_iou\_head}_i\).  We define
\[
p_i =
\begin{cases}
0, & u_i\leq 0,\\
2\sigma(u_i)-1, & u_i>0,
\end{cases}
\qquad
s_{\mathrm{B5},i}=p_i m_i.
\]
Non-finite required inputs fail closed.  B5 introduces no learned parameters
or additional runtime model; it applies this upstream-derived reliability
signal prospectively to the same frame-level write-side intervention used by
the other gated variants.

\subsection{Gate constraints}

The learned admission component is deliberately small and is constrained to
at most 50K trainable parameters.  The B2 instantiation used here contains
4,994 learned parameters across its two failure-typed heads.  No gradient is
propagated into SAM 3, and we use no LoRA adaptation, SAM fine-tuning,
detector modification, Perceiver compression, or replacement SAM
architecture.  Thus all trainable capacity is confined to the admission
gate rather than the segmentation substrate.

Identity similarity is evaluated under a fixed numerical protocol.  All
pointer cosine similarities are computed in FP32 with autocast disabled and
CUDA TF32 disabled, and with
\texttt{torch.set\_float32\_matmul\_precision("highest")}.  These settings
are held fixed across self-identity and competitor-relative identity
variants; numerical precision is therefore not another experimental factor.

\section{Evaluation Protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}

\subsection{Data and leakage boundaries}

We evaluate on MOSEv2~\cite{MOSEv2}.  The usable source partition contains
3,666 training videos; the released validation partition does not provide
the frame-by-frame masks required by our target-IoU, identity-theft, and
reappearance-event evaluation.  Final development and test cohorts are
therefore constructed from the training partition under video-level leakage
control, excluding videos already exposed during model or protocol
development.

Fresh DEV contains 40 videos and 233 frozen primary reappearance events.
It is used for write-rate matching before final TEST execution.  The primary
hard-stratum cohort, HARD\_TEST80, contains 80 videos and 506 frozen primary
events.  REPRESENTATIVE\_TEST40 contains 40 videos and 76 frozen primary
events and serves as a development-untouched external-validity anchor.
Cohort memberships are frozen before final TEST prediction generation.

\subsection{Endpoints}

A qualifying reappearance event follows a target-visibility gap of at least
five frames, where GT visibility requires a non-empty target mask.  Recovery
for POR@30 occurs when target IoU exceeds 0.5 within the first 30 evaluable
GT-visible frames after reappearance, subject to the frozen overlapping-event
truncation rule.  POR@30 is the proportion of qualifying events that recover
and is the sole primary endpoint.

The single primary research-question contrast is
\[
    \mathrm{POR@30}(\mathrm{B3\mbox{-}S})
    -
    \mathrm{POR@30}(\mathrm{B2})
\]
at the frozen headline operating rate \(r_\star=0.30\).  B3-R versus B3-S,
B3-R versus B2, B2 versus B1, B5 comparisons, and gated-method comparisons
with B0 are pre-specified secondary contrasts.  Because B0 remains at its
native write rate, B0 comparisons are practical-reference comparisons rather
than same-budget selector comparisons.

Identity-Theft Rate at 30 frames (ITR@30) is secondary.  A theft frame
satisfies both
\[
    \mathrm{target\ IoU}<0.3
    \quad\text{and}\quad
    \mathrm{max\ other\ IoU}>0.5.
\]
A theft episode requires at least five consecutive chronological video frames
satisfying this definition.  Within the POR@30-aligned event interval, a
qualifying reappearance event receives a theft indicator of one if at least
one such episode occurs and zero otherwise.  ITR@30 is the proportion of all
evaluated qualifying reappearance events with indicator one, including
events with no observed competitor overlap.

\subsection{Write-rate matching}

For video \(v\), let \(N_v\) be the number of eligible non-conditioning
frame-write opportunities and \(K_v\) the number physically admitted.
Conditioning and prompt frames are excluded, and one eligible frame counts as
one opportunity regardless of object count.  The physical frame-write rate
is \(K_v/N_v\), while threshold selection uses the pooled DEV rate
\[
    r_{\mathrm{pool}}
    =
    \frac{\sum_v K_v}{\sum_v N_v}.
\]

The common headline operating rate is selected on Fresh DEV using write rate
only; POR, ITR, and other tracking outcomes are not available to this
selection.  The frozen result is \(r_\star=0.30\), with headline thresholds
0.10 for B1, 0.10 for B2, 0.20 for B3-S, 0.20 for B3-R, and 0.70 for B5.
A method is considered matched to the target when its absolute DEV pooled
write-rate error is at most 0.02.

All thresholds and supported curve mappings are frozen before TEST and are
never searched, interpolated, extrapolated, refined, or retuned on TEST.
For a direct gated TEST comparison, if the absolute pooled write-rate
difference exceeds 0.02, we label that contrast \textsc{Rate-Mismatch} and
do not describe it as a matched-rate result.  B0 is reported separately at
its native physical write rate.

\subsection{Exact-budget neutral selectors}

Each gated method at the headline operating point is paired with an
outcome-independent timing control that has exactly the same physical write
budget in every video.  For a method with \(K_{m,v}\) admitted opportunities
among \(N_v\) eligible opportunities, the neutral control admits exactly
\(K_{m,v}\) opportunities.  When \(0<K_{m,v}<N_v\), chronologically index
eligible opportunities by \(0,\ldots,N_v-1\) and select
\[
    q_j =
    \left\lfloor
    \frac{(j+0.5)N_v}{K_{m,v}}
    \right\rfloor,
    \qquad
    j=0,\ldots,K_{m,v}-1.
\]
For \(K_{m,v}=0\) it selects none, and for \(K_{m,v}=N_v\) it selects all.
Thus
\[
    K_{\mathrm{neutral},v}=K_{\mathrm{method},v}
\]
with zero-frame per-video budget mismatch.

The selector uses no GT, gate score, prediction quality, POR, ITR, future
outcome, or object identity.  It is an evaluation control rather than a
deployable online gate.  Signal-versus-neutral comparisons therefore test
whether the signal-informed timing is preferable to an outcome-independent
schedule at the same realized write budget; they are diagnostics and do not
replace the pre-specified primary contrast.

\subsection{Statistical inference}

The video is the statistical cluster.  Primary uncertainty is quantified by
a paired video-clustered BCa 95\% confidence interval for the pooled
event-level POR@30 difference.  We use 50,000 bootstrap replicates with
deterministic seed 52.  Each replicate samples videos with replacement,
retains all event rows belonging to each sampled video, and uses the same
sampled video indices across compared methods.

BCa bias correction uses the midrank bootstrap position with finite-sample
clipping, and acceleration is computed by delete-one-video jackknife.  The
same clustered procedure is used for pre-specified secondary and
signal-versus-neutral contrasts.  The minimum practically important
hard-stratum POR@30 benefit is frozen at \(+0.08\).

\subsection{Frozen TEST procedure}

Final TEST execution follows a one-touch campaign.  Before the first TEST
trajectory can access TEST video frames or annotations, a persistent campaign
marker binds the run to the frozen repository commit, configuration hash, and
cohort memberships.  If an engineering interruption occurs after that marker
exists, only a resume under the identical frozen commit and configuration may
continue unfinished trajectories.  Partial TEST outcomes cannot change any
model, feature, threshold, operating rate, cohort, endpoint, or inference
choice.

HARD\_TEST80 executes the frozen supported write-rate curve points together
with the corresponding exact-budget neutral controls.  REPRESENTATIVE\_TEST40
executes the final headline variants and their headline neutral controls
only, consistent with its role as an external-validity anchor.  Negative,
null, harmful, positive, and inconclusive outcomes are all retained.

The pre-registered primary contrast is B3-S minus B2.  Its observed POR@30
difference is \(+0.007905\) (\(+0.791\) percentage points), with paired
video-clustered BCa 95\% interval
\([-0.002037,\,0.020882]\).  However, the realized write rates are 0.2906
for B3-S and 0.3233 for B2, an absolute difference of 0.03270.  This exceeds
the frozen 0.02 tolerance, so the primary contrast is
\textsc{Rate-Mismatch}; we therefore do not claim a matched-rate primary
effect from this comparison.

Among the pre-specified matched secondary contrasts, B2 exceeds B1 by
\(0.029644\) POR@30 (\(2.964\) percentage points), with BCa 95\% interval
\([0.006122,\,0.064302]\).  Their write-rate difference is 0.003346, within
the frozen tolerance.  This provides positive secondary evidence that the
learned quality--temporal gate improves post-occlusion recovery relative to
the matched manual quality--temporal rule.

\subsection{What do identity signals add?}

The clean matched identity-family comparison is B3-R minus B3-S.  Their
write-rate difference is 0.001756 and their POR@30 difference is exactly
0.000000, with BCa 95\% interval
\([-0.011287,\,0.014307]\).  Thus, the tested competitor-relative native
pointer signal does not establish an incremental recovery benefit beyond
the self-identity variant.

B3-S minus B2 cannot serve as a clean matched identity comparison on
HARD\_TEST80 because of the primary rate mismatch described above.  Likewise,
B3-R minus B2 has a write-rate difference of 0.03095 and is also
\textsc{Rate-Mismatch}.  The evidence therefore does not support a general
claim that identity information is ineffective; it supports only the
narrower conclusion that the tested native-pointer identity additions do not
establish incremental recovery benefit under the comparisons that remain
rate-compatible.

\subsection{Why write-rate control matters}

B0 achieves POR@30 of 0.7411 while writing at every eligible opportunity.
The gated variants write at substantially lower rates, approximately
0.29--0.32 at the headline operating point.  Consequently, raw B0-versus-gate
differences combine selector behavior with a large write-budget difference
and are reported only as practical-reference comparisons, not as controlled
same-budget effects.

B5 is rate-compatible with B2 at the headline point and differs by
\(-0.003953\) POR@30, with BCa 95\% interval
\([-0.019608,\,0.012500]\).  This interval includes zero and does not
establish an advantage for the DMS-lite write-side comparator over B2.

general monotone rule that either more or fewer memory writes improve
recovery.  The headline \(r_\star=0.30\) operating point was selected from

the most favorable TEST threshold.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The results support a narrow conclusion:
within the tested frozen SAM 3 substrate and evaluation protocol,
learned quality-temporal information provides the strongest demonstrated
basis for memory admission.  The matched B2-versus-B1 result shows that a
learned quality--temporal policy can improve recovery relative to the frozen
manual quality--temporal rule at a comparable physical write rate.  However,
the exact-budget neutral diagnostics do not establish that any tested
signal-informed policy is superior to an outcome-independent timing schedule
at the same per-video write budget.  The positive B2-versus-B1 result should
therefore be interpreted as a comparison between two frozen admission
policies, not as proof that signal-based timing is universally optimal.

The supported write-rate sweep reinforces this caution.  B2, B3-S, B3-R,
and B5 have non-monotonic POR@30 trajectories over their frozen supported
TEST points, while B1 decreases across its three supported points as write
rate increases.  Memory-write frequency alone therefore does not explain
the observed recovery behavior, and selecting a favorable TEST threshold
would give a misleading picture of policy quality.

The identity result is also bounded by signal availability.  In the
development-only EXP022 relational-signal census, valid generated identity
pointers were present on approximately 55.7\% of candidate rows across
26 videos, with substantial video-to-video heterogeneity.  The deployed
frozen routing therefore falls back from B3-R to B3-S and then to B2 when
the required identity information is unavailable.  Consequently, the
matched B3-R-versus-B3-S null result concerns the tested native SAM 3
pointer signals under this availability and fallback regime; it does not
establish that identity information, external re-identification features,
or other learned identity representations are generally unhelpful.

Identity-theft events are additionally low-frequency in the HARD\_TEST80
headline evaluation.  Depending on the policy, 16--30 of 506 qualifying
reappearance events satisfy the frozen ITR@30 theft-event criterion.
ITR@30 is therefore retained as a secondary endpoint with video-clustered
uncertainty, and sparse or near-zero theft counts are not promoted into
stronger identity conclusions.

The study is restricted to one frozen SAM 3 VOS substrate and MOSEv2, with
cohorts constructed from the MOSEv2 training partition because the released
validation split cannot support the required frame-level endpoint
construction.  The results therefore do not establish generalization to
other VOS architectures, datasets, memory organizations, or identity
representations.

Finally, EXP055 is explicitly post-defense, supplementary, and
nonconfirmatory.  Its full-video mIoU point estimates favor B0 over B2, but
the corresponding pooled and video-balanced confidence intervals include
zero.  This probe establishes neither a statistically reliable full-video
improvement nor degradation for B2 and does not modify the frozen
confirmatory conclusions.

They do not support the broader claims that memory gating universally
improves SAM 3, that identity information is generally unhelpful, or that
the tested gate improves ordinary full-video segmentation quality.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Memory admission should be evaluated as a controlled decision problem rather
than as an incidental reduction in write frequency.
Under matched write rates, our learned quality-temporal gate improves
post-occlusion recovery over a manual policy, while the tested native SAM 3
identity signals provide no established incremental recovery benefit.
The exact-budget controls further show why memory-write policies require
budget-aware evaluation before gains can be attributed to their signals.
